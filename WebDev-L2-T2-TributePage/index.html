Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

15 October 1931 - 27 July 2015

A Life of Purpose and Dedication

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam, commonly known as Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, was born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Growing up in a humble family, he sold newspapers in his early childhood to support his family while maintaining an insatiable curiosity for learning and mathematics. His relentless dedication laid the groundwork for an extraordinary journey that would fundamentally transform India’s defense capabilities and scientific landscape.

After graduating in physics and later studying aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology, Kalam joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and later the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). As the project director of India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III), he successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-earth orbit, marking India's arrival as a prominent space-faring nation.

Beyond his monumental contributions to the Pokhran-II nuclear tests and the Integrated Guided Missile Development Program—which earned him the revered moniker "Missile Man of India"—Dr. Kalam transitioned into public service. He served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Affectionately called the "People's President," he kept Rashtrapati Bhavan's doors open to children and common citizens, prioritizing education, youth empowerment, and national development above all protocol.

Even after his presidential tenure, Dr. Kalam dedicated his final years to teaching, writing books like Wings of Fire, and interacting with millions of students across universities. He passed away on July 27, 2015, while doing what he loved most: delivering a lecture to students at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong. His legacy continues to ignite young minds worldwide.

“You have to dream before your dreams can come true.”
